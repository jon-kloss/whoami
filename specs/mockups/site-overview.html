Building an AI Workshop for Kids Who Don't Know They're Learning

What happens when you give an 8-year-old access to AI game development tools and step back.

My nephews are 8 and 11. They play Minecraft and Fortnite. They don't care about programming, and they definitely don't care about AI. But they do care about making cool things.

The Premise

I wanted to build something my nephews could use without knowing they were learning to think like engineers. The constraint: everything has to feel like play, not homework.

Claude Code gave me a base to work from. Custom skills let me wrap complex workflows in simple commands. A kid types /game and gets a working Phaser.js game in their browser.

$ /game > What kind of game do you want to make? "a platformer where you're a cat" > Building your game... > Done! Opening in your browser.

What I Learned

Kids don't iterate the way developers do. They want the first thing to work, and if it doesn't, they move on. The workshop needed to produce working output on the first try, every time.

This forced a level of reliability I wouldn't have aimed for otherwise. Every edge case in the game generator was a potential moment where a kid loses interest. The error budget is zero.
