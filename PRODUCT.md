# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences visit this site in roughly equal measure:

- **Hiring managers and recruiters** evaluating Jon for engineering roles. They scan projects, skim the resume, and judge technical credibility within seconds. Speed and clarity matter.
- **Developer peers** who discover Jon through blog posts, open-source work, or community links. They want to learn, see how he thinks, and decide whether to follow or collaborate.

Both audiences form an opinion about competence from the craft of the site itself. A developer portfolio that looks generic undermines the message.

## Product Purpose

A personal developer portfolio and blog that demonstrates engineering skill through its own design and build quality. The site is proof of work: if the portfolio itself is well-crafted, the projects inside it are credible.

Pages: Home (hero + featured content), Blog (Markdown posts), Projects (curated highlights + GitHub repos), About, Resume/CV, Contact (form via third-party service).

Success: visitors remember the site, share blog posts, and come back.

## Brand Personality

**Bold. Sharp. Confident.**

- Speaks with authority, not arrogance
- Shows rather than tells — the craft speaks for itself
- Direct, concise copy — no filler, no padding
- Treats the reader as an intelligent peer

## Anti-references

- **Generic template portfolios** — the identical card grid + hero section + "Hi, I'm [name]" formula. If it could be anyone's site, it's no one's site.
- **Corporate SaaS aesthetic** — Stripe-clone gradients, purple-to-blue heroes, identical rounded cards, cream backgrounds with soft shadows.
- **Cluttered / information overload** — trying to show everything at once. No wall-of-skills badges, no crowded sidebars.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is the design** — Typography, spacing, and rhythm ARE the visual identity. No decoration for its own sake. Every element earns its space.
2. **Editorial confidence** — Treat each page like a magazine spread. Bold type hierarchy, generous negative space, sections that flow into each other.
3. **Show the craft** — The portfolio proves technical skill through its own build quality. Smooth transitions, thoughtful interactions, fast performance.
4. **Sharp, not loud** — Bold doesn't mean noisy. Confidence comes from precision and restraint in the right places, not from visual volume everywhere.
5. **One strong impression** — A visitor should remember one thing about this site. Not everything — one thing, clearly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA compliance (minimum 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text)
- Full keyboard navigation
- Screen reader support with semantic HTML and ARIA where needed
- Respects prefers-reduced-motion for users who disable animations
- Functional without JavaScript for core content (blog posts, about, resume)
